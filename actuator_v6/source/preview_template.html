Partition revision / 28 October 2026

A printed barrier between the mechanism and the circuit.

V6-S + V6-L

What changed

  • A continuous 1.00 mm wall is part of the printed body, covering the full PCB length. No holes are needed for magnetic sensing.
  • The PCB moves outward by 1.15 mm. The body section is now 16.10 × 14.70 mm.
  • PCB components have 0.30 mm nominal clearance to the partition, or 0.25 mm with the board shifted fully inward in its slot.
  • The carrier retains its 0.15 mm guide clearance to plastic. Its full travel envelope is 1.45 mm away from the components.

Magnet and travel

  • Use an axially magnetised N35 Ø3 × 2 mm magnet. The original Ø2 × 2 mm magnet is too weak in a modelled tolerance case at the increased distance.
  • The Hall sensors are repositioned; the nominal strokes remain 40 mm and 85 mm.
  • Mechanical overtravel is now 3 mm at each end. All 54 magnetic tolerance cases trigger, with 1.35 mm minimum predicted clearance to the hard stop.
  • Pin centres are 120–160 mm for S and 165–250 mm for L. The body and end caps are revised parts.

Checks and remaining work

  • The CAD build checks wall continuity, clearance across the carrier’s full travel envelope, sampled assembly interference, wire routing and watertight print meshes.
  • Both PCB layouts are regenerated and checked, including Gerber readback.
  • The circuit topology is unchanged. The existing nine circuit simulations use an assumed motor and 45 mm example stroke.
  • Print fit, strength, temperature and real end-stop operation still require physical tests. Nearby steel is excluded from the magnetic model.

The barrier protects components from direct contact; it does not remove the need to check the printed sliding fit. Use ordinary unfilled PETG. A larger magnet, revised caps and revised PCB layouts are required for this partition version. The stop model predicts a unit-to-unit spread of −2.15 to +1.65 mm about each nominal end position; quoted strokes are nominal, not calibrated travel guarantees.

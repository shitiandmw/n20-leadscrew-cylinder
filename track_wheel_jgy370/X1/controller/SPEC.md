# X1 control system: spec C1, issue 4

**Status:** the requirements for the main board, which is now designed to them: the schematic is complete and checked, in [main_board/DESIGN.md](main_board/DESIGN.md). The PCB layout is in progress.

**Issue 4 (2026-11-05, PCB layout):** the board's floor plan moved two things. The tracks' JST-XH 4-pin went from the front edge to the inboard edge (C1, C2), and the module went to the front end of the outboard edge (C3).

**Issue 3 (2026-11-05):** the user rejected the handle's appearance ("terrible") and parked it to focus on the excavator's control circuit. Section 7 keeps only what the main board depends on, which is the link and the control behaviour. The issue-2 handle sheet and its face checks were removed from the drawings. Open item 2 (DRV8214) is closed for rev A.

**Issue 2 (2026-11-05; its handle layout is parked by issue 3, its control behaviour stays):** the handle followed the Sand/Lab phone remote, at the user's request ("refer to the mobile app design; put the two tracks' controls where both index fingers can reach"):
- the same two labelled joysticks;
- per-track ↑/↓ hold buttons, now on the shoulders under the index fingers;
- the 20–100 % movement-speed slider;
- an EMERGENCY STOP that only RESUME clears;
- the app's status line on the OLED.

As a result:
- the analog triggers, D-pad, ABXY buttons, speed ranges and travel mode of issue 1 are gone;
- the link timeout is now the app's 350 ms (was 300).

**Covers:**
- the main control board in the X1 upper body;
- the handle's link and control behaviour (the handle itself is parked, section 7);
- the radio link between them;
- the firmware on both;
- what is reserved for the later 4G remote.

**Drawings:** `X1_control_spec_C1.pdf`, generated by `source/draw_control_spec.py`. It has two sheets:
1. system block diagram;
2. main board concept in the left bay.

The script also writes `X1_control_spec_C1_checks.json`. It recomputes the budgets below, and it reads the board constants from `../source/x1_body.py` so that they cannot drift.

Coordinates are the X1 machine frame in mm: X forward, Y to the left, Z up.

## 2. What the board drives

| Channel | Motor | Supply | Current limit | Ramp | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Left track | JGY-370-1260 12 V 200:1 (rated 0.2 A, stall 1.3 A) | VBAT | 1.0 A | 0.3 s | J9 README → Motor choice; open item 7 (reverse stall against the idler) |
| 2 Right track | same; the motor is mounted mirrored, so the firmware inverts its direction | VBAT | 1.0 A | 0.3 s | X1 decisions → Right track |
| 3 Swing | same motor; the worm gear self-locks | VBAT | 0.6 A | **0.4 s, mandatory** | TURNTABLE.md: 204 N on a pinion tooth without the ramp, 35 N with it |
| 4 Boom left | V6-U60 two-wire actuator (N20 6 V, 7 V maximum), with its own Hall end stops and a 0.12 A PTC | 6 V | 0.5 A (backstop) | 0.1 s | actuator_v6 README; simulation/results.json |
| 5 Boom right | same | 6 V | 0.5 A | 0.1 s | |
| 6 Stick | same | 6 V | 0.5 A | 0.1 s | |
| 7 Bucket | same | 6 V | 0.5 A | 0.1 s | |

- The two track motors reach the board through the 6-way slip ring. They use 4 of its rings; 2 are spare.
- An actuator runs at about 0.12 A and stalls at about 0.35 A (simulated). Its own PTC trips on a jam in about 1.6 s. The board's 0.5 A limit only guards the wiring.
- Actuator speed is 0.82–1.25 mm/s, so a full 60 mm stroke takes 50–75 s. That is set by the actuator, not by the board.
- **PWM:** 20 kHz, drive and brake (slow decay). The V6 end-stop circuit passes simulation at this setting (`actuator_v6/electronics/simulation/results.json`, case `pwm20k_brake`).

## 3. Main board requirements

### Power

- **P1. Input:** XT30 from the toolbox 12 V link. The path is battery → BMS → 5 A fuse → main switch → link (BODY.md → Wiring).
  - Reverse-polarity protection with a P-MOSFET.
  - A TVS diode and bulk capacitors for motor transients.
- **P2. Rails:**
  - VBAT, 9.0–12.6 V, unregulated: the track and swing drivers.
  - **6 V buck, 3 A:** 4 actuators × 0.5 A limit = 2.0 A.
  - **5 V buck, 3 A:** logic, lamps and buzzer (about 0.6 A), plus 2 A kept in reserve for the expansion header (section 8).
  - **3.3 V low-noise regulator (LDO), at least 0.5 A:** the ESP32-S3 and the analog references.
- **P3. Battery voltage:** read through a divider into the ADC.
- **P4. Budget (checked in the JSON):**
  - Worst case, every channel at its limit at 9.6 V: **4.2 A**, under the 5 A fuse.
  - Adding a 4G stick later (5 V, 2 A) would raise this to about 5.4 A. The fuse is reviewed in that phase.

### Motor drivers

- **M1.** One driver type for all 7 channels: **TI DRV8876** (HTSSOP-16, 4.5–37 V, 3.5 A peak, LCSC C575551).
  - It has its own current regulation.
  - Its current-sense output, IPROPI, is a 1000 µA/A mirror.
- **M2.** One shared 2.5 V reference sets the regulation threshold. Each channel's IPROPI resistor sets its limit:
  - 2.5 kΩ for 1.0 A;
  - 4.17 kΩ for 0.6 A;
  - 5.0 kΩ for 0.5 A.
  - At each limit the IPROPI voltage is 2.5 V, inside the ESP32-S3 ADC range at 12 dB attenuation. An actuator running at 0.12 A gives 0.6 V.
- **M3.** Phase/enable mode: one PWM pin and one direction pin per channel. The sleep line is shared, and the fault line is shared open-drain.

### Controller

- **U1. ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N8:** the module with the printed antenna, no PSRAM.
- **Preliminary pin budget:** 33 of the module's 36 GPIO.

  | Use | GPIO |
  |---|---|
  | ADC1 inputs: 7 currents + battery | 8 |
  | Motor PWM + direction | 14 |
  | Sleep and fault | 2 |
  | Lamp 1, lamp 2, buzzer | 3 |
  | Status LED | 1 |
  | USB | 2 |
  | Expansion UART | 2 |
  | BOOT | 1 |

- **U2. USB-C** for flashing and logs, through the S3's native USB. No USB-to-serial chip.
- **U3.** A status LED for the bench, and BOOT and RESET buttons.

### Outputs and connectors

- **O1. Lamps:** 2 outputs at 5 V, switched on the low side, PWM-dimmable, 100 mA each. They feed the roof work lamps and the toolbox lamp, all modelled on the body.
- **O2. Buzzer** on the board, for the travel alarm and warnings.
- **C1. Front edge**, where the 12 V link arrives over T101: XT30 power in.
  - *Issue 4 (PCB layout):* the tracks' JST-XH 4-pin moved to the inboard edge. With both connectors on the front edge there was no room for the input FET and the two track drivers behind them.
- **C2. Inboard edge**, from front to rear:
  - JST-XH 4-pin for both tracks. This matches the slip-ring plug in swing concept R2. It sits at the front end, next to where the slip-ring leads arrive over T101.
  - 4 × JST-XH 2-pin: boom L, boom R, stick, bucket;
  - JST-XH 2-pin for the swing motor. Its leads arrive from the rear notch.
  - JST-PH 3-pin for the lamps;
  - JST-PH 4-pin for the expansion header.
- **C3. Outboard edge:** USB-C at X −98 and the module's antenna.
  - *Issue 4:* the module sits at the front end of this edge (X −78.5 to −60.5), with the antenna at the edge. From the module's side edge, it is 15 mm to the USB-C shell and 14 mm to the front boss screw (Espressif recommends 15 mm). The copper keep-out under and beyond the antenna is ±22 mm about the module centre; KiCad's footprint uses ±24 mm, which does not fit between the boss and the USB-C.

### Mechanical

- **B1. Board:** 96 × 64 × 1.6 mm, 4 layers. Holes are Ø2.7 at 4 mm from the corners, on the existing bosses (`BOARD` in `x1_body.py`).
  - Components on the top side only; the board sits 2.5 mm above the bay floor.
- **B2. Height:** components and mated plugs at most 20 mm above the board. The limit is Z 134; the board top is at Z 111.6, which leaves 22.4 mm.
- **B3. Antenna:** keep copper away from the antenna end at the outboard edge. The steel shot sits at least 15 mm behind it. Check the range on the bench.
- **B4. USB slot:** the B102 skirt slot moves from Z 123.3 (a devkit on headers) to the board connector's centre, about Z 113.2. This is a body CAD change, and the body checks are run again in the CAD phase.
- **B5. Plug clearance:** the connector orientation (top or side entry) and plug clearance in the bay are fixed in the CAD phase.

## 4. Control scheme (decision D5)

The control mapping copies the Sand/Lab phone remote (`excavator_app/client/main.js`, `excavator_app/docs/remote.png`). The labels are the app's. Where the inputs sit on the handle is open while the handle is parked (section 7).

| In the app | On the handle | Motion | Channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 STICK / SLEW pad, up / down | left stick, forward / back: STICK OUT / STICK IN | stick out / in | 6 |
| 01 pad, left / right | left stick, left / right: L / R | swing left / right | 3 |
| 02 BOOM / BUCKET pad, up / down | right stick, forward / back: LOWER / RAISE | boom down / up | 4 + 5 |
| 02 pad, left / right | right stick, left / right: CURL / OPEN | bucket curl / open | 7 |
| LEFT TRACK ↑ / ↓ (hold) | left shoulder, upper ↑ / lower ↓ (hold), left index finger | left track forward / reverse | 1 |
| RIGHT TRACK ↑ / ↓ (hold) | right shoulder, upper ↑ / lower ↓ (hold), right index finger | right track forward / reverse | 2 |
| Movement speed slider, 20–100 %, 5 % steps, default 65 % | slider, same range and steps | scales every axis | all |
| EMERGENCY STOP | red button, centre | everything stops, latched | all |
| Resume operation | RESUME button | clears the stop | — |

- **ISO pattern,** as in the app. All axes can move at the same time.
- **Sticks:** proportional, with the app's 12 % dead band.
- **Tracks:** hold-to-travel buttons, as in the app. They run at the movement speed, and the 0.3 s ramp smooths the start and stop.
  - Both buttons of one pair pressed at once: that track stops.
  - A pivot turn is left ↑ with right ↓.
- **Who does what, as in the app:** the handle sends the raw inputs and the speed setting. The machine scales and ramps them.
- **No brakes:** the leadscrews and the worm self-lock. A centred input brakes the motor and the axis holds.
- **Telemetry the app shows that the machine cannot:** boom stroke, stick and bucket strokes, and bucket load. The real machine has no position or load sensors. Open item 2 could give stroke estimates later.

### Boom pair (channels 4 and 5)

The V6 has no position feedback, so the two sides are kept together from current alone:

1. **Same command** to both channels.
2. **Load balancing:** the side drawing more current is ahead and carrying more of the boom. Its duty is trimmed down slowly, by at most 15 %, until the currents match. This also helps keep each actuator under its PTC hold current of 0.12 A.
3. **Re-sync at the ends:** when one side's current drops to zero (its end stop has opened), the other side keeps being driven until it stops too.
4. **Re-sync from the handle:** one button drives both sides to the retract end stops.
5. **Imbalance fault:** if the difference stays over 40 mA for 3 s, the boom stops and the handle reports a fault. Both values are starting points, to be tuned on the bench.

**Limit:** this keeps the sides close, not exactly equal. A possible later upgrade is in open item 2.

## 5. Safety

- **S1. Link loss:** no valid packet for 350 ms (the app's input watchdog) stops every channel. The swing ramps down over its 0.4 s to protect the pinion; the others stop at once.
- **S2. EMERGENCY STOP on the handle:** latching, as in the app. It stops everything, with the swing ramping as in S1. Only RESUME clears it, and only with all inputs centred, matching the app's "Resume operation".
- **S3. Power-up:** the machine stays disarmed until a paired handle connects with all inputs centred. Nothing moves on boot.
- **S4. Stall cut-out:** a 12 V channel held at 90 % or more of its limit for 2 s is switched off, and stays off until its input returns to centre. This protects the closed track motor pods (J9 open item 8).
- **S5. Low battery:**
  - warning at 3.5 V per cell (10.5 V), shown on the handle with vibration;
  - motors stop at 3.2 V per cell (9.6 V).
  - Both are measured on a 2 s filtered value, so that load sag doesn't trip them. The BMS cut-off remains the last line.
- **S6. Driver faults:** the fault line (overcurrent, overheating, undervoltage) stops the machine and is reported.

## 6. Radio link

- **L1. ESP-NOW,** paired by MAC address, encrypted, and only with the paired peer. Pairing is started from the handle's menu.
- **L2. Traffic:**
  - handle → machine: 50 Hz, with 4 stick axes, the speed setting, the button states (4 track buttons, EMERGENCY STOP, RESUME, lamps, horn, re-sync), a sequence number and a CRC;
  - machine → handle: 10 Hz telemetry, with battery, faults, channel states and signal strength.
- **L3. Fixed Wi-Fi channel** (configurable, default 1). The later 4G phase can then share the radio by fixing its hotspot to the same channel.

## 7. Handle: parked (issue 3)

The issue-2 handle concept (a gamepad copying the phone remote) was rejected on its appearance and is **parked**. Its design restarts after the main board. Its outline and proportions will come from a real, cited product (dimensioned drawings of a gamepad or RC transmitter), and the function diagram will be kept separate from the appearance.

What stays fixed, because the main board and its firmware depend on it:
- **H1. Link interface:** section 6, unchanged. The handle sends 4 stick axes, the speed setting and the button states (4 track buttons, EMERGENCY STOP, RESUME, lamps, horn, re-sync) at 50 Hz. It receives 10 Hz telemetry.
- **H2. Controller:** an ESP32-S3, so that ESP-NOW is the same on both ends.
- **H3. Control behaviour:** sections 4 and 5. ISO mapping as the app, hold-to-travel track buttons, the 20–100 % speed setting, and EMERGENCY STOP latched until RESUME.

## 8. 4G remote: deferred (decision D6)

Not designed or implemented in this phase. The only provisions are these:

- **X1.** A JST-PH 4-pin expansion header: 5 V (up to 2 A), GND, and UART TX and RX at 3.3 V. It carries a later serial link.
- **X2.** The 5 V buck is sized for that 2 A.
- **X3.** The fixed radio channel (L3) and the ESP32-S3's own Wi-Fi allow a later Wi-Fi link with no hardware change.
- **X4.** The right bay of B103 is now empty, about 200 × 75 × 28 mm, and can take a 4G stick later.
- **X5.** Control priority stays with the handle; the remote takes over only through an explicit handover from the handle.

## 9. Firmware

- **F1. Toolchain:** Arduino-ESP32 3.x (ESP-IDF 5 underneath), one project each for the machine and the handle. They share a header that defines the link packets.
- **F2. Machine:**
  - channel table: limits, ramps, direction inversion;
  - input mapping;
  - boom-pair logic;
  - the safety rules S1–S6;
  - telemetry;
  - a USB serial console for calibration and logs.
- **F3. Handle:**
  - input sampling and calibration (centre, the app's 12 % dead band, end points);
  - the speed slider in 5 % steps;
  - menu and pairing;
  - the OLED status line and vibration.
- **F4. Tests on the host, no hardware**, in the same style as `actuator_v5/electronics/protection_logic_test.cpp`: ramps, mapping (the same axis signs as the app), both track buttons pressed, boom balancing and re-sync, link loss, EMERGENCY STOP and RESUME, stall cut-out and low battery.

## 10. How each stage will be checked

- **Schematic:** the connections are rebuilt from the drawing and compared with the netlist and the firmware pin map, as in `actuator_v6/source/build_schematic.py`.
- **PCB:** design-rule check, Gerber read-back, and a JLCPCB parts list and placement file. The stock of every part is checked at LCSC.
- **3D:** a board STEP placed in the X1 body. Then the body checks run again, including the USB plug through the moved slot and the connectors' plug clearance.
- **Firmware:** the host tests (F4) pass, and both projects compile.
- **Not checkable here:** radio range, real motor currents, IPROPI accuracy at low current, heat. These go on the bench bring-up list.

## 11. Open items and risks

1. **Current-sense accuracy at about 0.1 A.** The DRV8876 IPROPI has a fixed offset at low current (the datasheet's error band applies below 0.15 A). The boom balancing depends on it. Plan: zero each channel at rest, then check on the bench.
2. **Possible upgrade for the boom pair: closed for rev A.**
   - The TI DRV8214 (motor supply up to 11 V, I²C) counts commutation ripple and so gives relative motor position without a sensor.
   - The rule was to switch only if two checks passed: LCSC stock, and proof that the ripple survives the V6's PTC and MOSFET path. The second needs a simulation or a bench test, which has not been done.
   - Rev A therefore keeps one driver type, the DRV8876, for all seven channels. Its I²C interface would also need two GPIO, and all 36 are now used.
   - The DRV8214 remains a rev B option if the bench shows the current balance is not good enough.
3. The DRV8876 is an LCSC "extended" part, which carries a one-time fee at JLCPCB. Check its stock before ordering.
4. Radio range from inside the PETG body, near the steel shot. Test on the bench.
5. Connector orientation and plug clearance in the bay (B5). Fix in CAD.
6. Handle ergonomics and appearance: parked with the handle (section 7).

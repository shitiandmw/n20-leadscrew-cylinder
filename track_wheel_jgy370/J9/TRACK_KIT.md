# Track kit: what to print and what to buy for two J9 tracks

Two J9 tracks, revision F (the tracks of the [complete X1 excavator](../../excavator_complete/README.md)). Generated by `source/build_track_kit.py` from the current STL files and the CAD scene, which it cross-checks against this list ([track_kit_report.json](track_kit_report.json)); the same rows are in [track_kit.csv](track_kit.csv). The subsystem lists stay authoritative: [J9 BOM](BOM.md), [X1 BOM](../X1/BOM.md).

- **Print:** 30 parts plus 68 track shoes, from 11 STL files (9 designs; the H-frame and the housing come as a left and a mirrored right file).
- **Buy:** 104 standard parts, plus the chain hardware (68 M3 x 60 screws, 68 sleeves, 68 nyloc nuts).
- **Installation animation:** [install_animation.html](install_animation.html). Open it offline in any browser. It plays the fitting order step by step, with the parts, tools and checks for each step, and has the same parts list in its "Parts list" tab.
- **Parts showcase:** [parts_showcase.html](parts_showcase.html). Every part to print or buy in 3D, offline: turn it, see it on the print bed or as fitted, cut it open, read its dimensions on the model (each checked against the STL or CAD mesh) and its material and print settings, and find it in the track.
- **Status:** nothing has been printed or assembled yet. The fits, strength and powered running are nominal CAD only (see the end of this page).
- **Shoe revision B (2026-11-14).** The pre-print review found three faults in the earlier shoe: the pins could slide sideways until an E-clip was under a roller flange, neighbouring links were 0.045 mm apart, and the sprocket teeth had 0.2 mm of side clearance between the ears. The shoe now takes an M3 x 60 screw and a nyloc nut on seat bosses, keeps 0.6 mm between neighbours and gives the teeth 0.7 mm a side. A J910 printed from a file older than 2026-11-14 is the earlier shoe: do not use it. The H-frame and the yoke changed the same day (lock-pin hole, deeper insert bores, wider roller bosses, more side room in the grooves).

### Print notes

- **Bed size.** The H-frame and the housing are the long parts; everything else fits any bed. Measured on the STL footprints:
  - J901 H-frame: 220 x 220: fits diagonally, turned 45°, 4.8 mm spare per side; 250 x 210: fits diagonally, turned 37.5°, 10.3 mm spare per side; 256 x 256: fits diagonally, turned 45°, 22.8 mm spare per side; 300 x 300: fits straight, 12.6 mm spare per side.
  - J909 housing: 220 x 220: does not fit (2.4 mm short); 250 x 210: fits diagonally, turned 37.5°, 4.3 mm spare per side; 256 x 256: fits diagonally, turned 45°, 16.8 mm spare per side; 300 x 300: fits straight, 6.6 mm spare per side.
  - X101 (optional): 220 x 220: does not fit (55 mm short); 250 x 210: does not fit (25 mm short); 256 x 256: does not fit (19 mm short); 300 x 300: fits straight, 12.5 mm spare per side.
  - Diagonal placements leave little room for a brim or the purge line; check the slicer preview.
- **Filament.** All the parts for two tracks are 1767 cm³, 2.24 kg of PETG if printed fully solid (the upper bound). With the settings above a rough shell estimate (not a slicer result) is 1.79 kg, plus support material, most of it under the H-frames' inner walls. Where the design gives no infill (H-frame, idler, housing) the estimate assumes 4 walls and 40 % (housing: 3 walls, 15 %). Sliced on 2026-11-14 (PrusaSlicer 2.9.2, 0.2 mm layers, 256 mm bed, these settings with 5 walls and 20 % for the H-frames): about 96 hours and 1.76 kg for both tracks, 0.20 kg of it support. Three 1 kg spools leave room for the fit-check prints and a failed print; check your own slicer total.
- **Print the fit checks first.** The bearing seats, the plug journal and the pin bores are nominal sizes that have not been printed yet. Before the long frames, print four shoes, one roller, one idler wheel, one spacer, one plug and one sprocket, and check them against the real parts:
  - four shoes joined with the real screws, sleeves and nuts: every joint swings under its own weight, and wrapped round the sprocket the teeth enter between the ears without help;
  - one roller: both 624ZZ press in flush without cracking the seat;
  - one idler wheel: each 608ZZ slides in by hand through its Ø22.6 bore and presses home against the central web, 13 mm below the face (trim any bridge sag on the bed side first);
  - the plug in a 6800ZZ inner ring, and a J907 spacer on the Ø8 pin;
  - the sprocket: the coupling flange (Ø32) seats in its Ø32.4 recess and the centre pad slides into the Ø6 bore.
  - If a fit is off, correct the slicer's hole compensation, not the CAD. Then print the left H-frame and check its 6800ZZ seat, the motor counterbores and the Ø16.4 spring bore (with the J908 piston) before printing the right one.
- **Left and right.** Print J901_H_frame.stl and J909_housing.stl from J9/stl for the left track and the _right files from X1/stl for the right track. Do not mirror a file in the slicer instead: the _right files are the checked revision F mirrors.

## 4. Installation order

The animation plays these steps; each step there lists its parts, tools and checks. The order is the X1 order, which also works on the bench: housing before the sprocket, and the rear sprocket and chain last, because on the chassis the joint nuts are reached from inside the track. If you build a track on the bench first and mount it on X101 later, take its chain, sprocket and journal plug off again for the joints.

1. **Prepare the H-frame**: Four M3 heat-set inserts into the housing standoffs on the inboard side; the 6800ZZ pressed into the outer wall.
2. **Motor**: The JGY-370 seats in the recess on the inboard face of the inner plate; four M3 x 6 go in from inside the H.
3. **Road and support rollers**: Two 624ZZ in each of the seven rollers. Road rollers go in from below, support rollers from above; M4 x 70 from the inboard side, nyloc nut outboard.
4. **Tensioner: spring, yoke and idler**: The idler, on two 608ZZ, sits between the yoke arms on the Ø8 x 55 pin. The spring goes into the guide bore from the front, then the yoke slides into the wall grooves (the frame's outboard half is cut away here to show the spring).
5. **Housing over the motor**: Motor leads through the Ø5.5 port in the pod nose and soldered on; the housing slides on toward the inner wall and takes four M3 x 8 button heads.
6. **Mount both tracks on X101** (only when mounting on X101): Both tracks upright on 340 mm centres. The bare X101 goes 80 mm straight down into the four seats; two M4 x 25 per joint, washers and nuts outside the plate.
7. **Sprocket and coupling**: The flange coupling bolts into the sprocket on the bench. The pair goes in between the walls from the rear, 9.6 mm outboard, then slides onto the D-shaft.
8. **Journal plug**: J903 goes in from outside through the 6800ZZ into the sprocket's socket; one M3 x 50 clamps plug and sprocket to the shaft end.
9. **Track chain**: The chain goes on in one piece, like a real track. On the bench each joint is made in turn: next link pushed on along the strip, sleeve dropped in between the inner ears, M3 x 60 screw through, nyloc nut on and backed off. A dummy pin holds the last sleeve while the strip is wrapped round the sprocket and, with the idler locked back at its stop, round the idler. On top the last screw pushes the dummy out; then the lock screw comes out and the spring takes up the slack.
10. **First run**: One slow circuit first, then both directions with the track lifted and the current limited to about 1 A.

### Fitting the chain (step 9)

The chain is not threaded on link by link round the wheels. It goes on in one piece, as a real track does; step 9 of the animation shows each move:

1. On the bench, join the 34 links into one straight strip, grousers down. For each joint: lay the next link down just ahead of the strip and push it back until its outer plates straddle the inner ears of the last link (the rounded plate ends slide into the hollowed ones, so it only goes on along the strip). Drop a sleeve in between those inner ears from above: at Ø6 it cannot pass the Ø3.2 holes, so it always goes in before its screw. Push an M3 x 60 screw through boss, plate, ear, sleeve, ear, plate and boss: it slides like a pin until its head rests on the seat boss. Run an M3 nyloc nut onto the tail until it touches the other boss, then back it off 1/6 turn: the joint must swing under its own weight. Put every head on the same side: they go outboard.
2. At the far end of the strip, drop the last sleeve between the free inner ears and push the Ø3 x 24 dummy pin through ears and sleeve until it sits inside both ears. On the top run those ears open downward, out of reach, and the dummy keeps the sleeve in. This joint stays open.
3. Lift the track, lay the strip under it and set the track down on the middle of the strip: the road rollers sit on it and the sprocket teeth drop between the sleeves. The open ends stick out behind the sprocket and ahead of the idler.
4. Wrap the rear end up round the sprocket, a sleeve in every tooth gap, and lay it forward over the support rollers.
5. Push the idler back to its recoil stop (4.2 mm behind its running position, where the yoke arms meet the groove ends); the spring pushes back with about 104 N. Drop a spare track screw into the Ø3.2 lock-pin hole in the outer wall: it goes 8 mm into the yoke and holds the idler there. Wrap the front end up round the idler and back over the top, held up in a low arch so it lands a few millimetres short of the rear end.
6. Press the arch down: the front end slides along the run until its inner ears (sleeve and dummy pin inside) are between the rear end's outer plates. The ends meet on top between the two support rollers, where both sides are clear of the frame, the housing and X101.
7. Push the last screw in from outboard. It pushes the dummy pin out on the inboard side: catch it. Run the nut on and back it off 1/6 turn.
8. Pull the lock screw out of the wall: the spring pushes the idler forward until the chain is tight, at about its 25.8 mm fitted length and 60-77 N, depending on the play in the 34 joints.

- **Check:** The chain is a fixed loop of 34 x 20 mm. If the ends will not meet with the idler at its stop, look for a stiff link or a binding yoke; do not force the screw.
- **Check:** Before closing the last joint, the dummy pin must sit inside both inner ears (24 mm in a 27.4 mm span): if it stands out, push it back, or the outer plates will not pass over the ears.
- **Check:** No nut may clamp: every joint swings freely. Heads and nuts sit 2 mm outboard of the roller flanges and must never touch them.
- **Check:** Released, the idler must come forward on its own and the chain must lie on every wheel. Press the idler back by hand: it must recoil and return freely.

## 5. Still to verify on the real parts

- **Fits:** the 6800ZZ, 624ZZ and 608 press fits, the Ø9.95 plug in the 6800ZZ, the Ø32.4 coupling recess, the Ø15.6 piston in the Ø16.4 bore.
- **Motor screw holes:** 3 mm of thread engagement in the gearbox is assumed. Measure the real holes.
- **Coupling set screw:** one set screw, no torque rating on a D-flat. Mark it, and recheck after the first runs and after any stall.
- **Reverse stall:** limit the driver to about 1.0 A; at the full 1.3 A stall the idler has almost no margin.
- **Heat:** the pod is closed. Feel the motor pod during the first runs and do not hold a stall.
- **Loads and wear:** no load rating is claimed; the vehicle mass is not specified.
